A cheap, benchmark-free safety score for open-weight checkpoints would let a platform triage models without holding harmful text. We report the fourth iteration of an attempt to build one from the act side, and it is mainly a report on what makes such measurements fragile. Two positive results from our own previous iteration are retracted, each by the experiment its own limitations section demanded, and what replaces them is a set of measurements that hold. First, scaling a lexical-invariance refit of a published activation-based safety scanner from 19 checkpoints over 7 weight lineages to 52 over 28 lineages and 11 architecture families collapses its criterion-validity gain from +0.296 to +0.099 with a confidence interval covering zero; an independently authored paraphrase set gives -0.152; and the effect is localised exactly, with the archived 19 members reproducing +0.2963 while the 33 new ones give -0.016. Second, measuring detection and induction in both roles of the same five axes on 30 checkpoints, scored on each model's own spontaneous text rather than on an archived, partly steered item pool, reverses our previously reported within-axis dissociation: 20 members READ, one is ambiguous, nine are undefined for want of spontaneous refusals, and zero are at chance, with the two roles correlated at rho = 0.629 [0.465, 0.803] over 70 (member, axis) pairs. Abliteration removes the refusals to be read, not the axis's ability to read them. Third, on fluency-screened text at matched axis-contrast units the canonical axis induces 0.747 any-refusal against a token-disjoint paraphrase axis's 0.028, which sits below the 0.146 floor a random direction sets on the same population. We also report two empirical nulls -- a matched random direction induces refusal on 7 of 30 checkpoints and reads at anything but 0.500 -- and show that the aggregation unit alone moves this study's correlations by a median 0.238 and flips 5 of 16 signs.
