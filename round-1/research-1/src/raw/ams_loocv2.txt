C. Leave-One-Out Cross-Validation of Thresholds
The reference thresholds in Table I (PASS >3.5σ, CRIT-
ICAL <2.0σ) were originally derived as midpoint heuristics
on this 14-model set, raising a calibration-evaluation coupling
concern (see also Section VIII-D). To assess generalization,
we perform leave-one-out cross-validation (LOOCV): for each
held-out model, we recompute the midpoint thresholds using
only the remaining 13 models and classify the held-out model
with those thresholds. Two calibration rules are evaluated: (a)
using σharmful only, and (b) using the minimum σ across the
three concepts (worst-concept rule).
Both rules achieve identical 71% accuracy (10/14). The
PASS threshold range across folds is [2.97, 4.55] under rule
(a) and [2.97, 4.19] under rule (b). The four misclassifications
are informative rather than random:

The threshold-rule generalization is therefore tight on the
canonical cases (clean training removal, clean Dolphin-style
fine-tunes) and loose on the boundary cases that activation-
only σ does not separate well from instruction-tuned models.
We report 71% as the exploratory LOOCV accuracy and note
that the misses are structurally explained, not noise.
D. Bootstrap Confidence Intervals on σ
A single σ value reported in Table I comes from one
specific set of 16 contrastive prompt pairs. A different set of
16 pairs would yield a slightly different σ. To quantify how
much σ varies across different pair sets, we use the bootstrap:
we repeatedly resample our 16 pairs with replacement (1000
times) and recompute σ for each resample. The 2.5th and
97.5th percentiles of the resulting distribution give us a 95%
confidence interval (CI) on σ. No new forward passes through
the model are needed; we resample the cached activation
projections.
Across all 42 cells (14 models × 3 concepts), the median
CI width is 3.36σ—meaningfully wide. Of the 42 cells, 26
(62%) have a 95% CI that crosses the 3.5σ PASS reference
threshold, and 14 (33%) cross the 2.0σ CRITICAL threshold.
In other words, the PASS/CRITICAL classification of many
models is statistically uncertain at the boundaries.
A second observation: the σ estimator systematically reports
values higher than the bootstrap-median, because it selects the
direction that maximizes separation on the specific sample of
pairs available. For Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct on harmful content,
the point estimate is 8.37σ but the 95% CI is [3.64, 7.95]—
the reported point estimate lies above the upper bound of its
own CI. This upward bias is consistent across cells with high
reported σ.
We recommend reporting the CI lower bound, not the
single-run point estimate, as the operationally honest summary
statistic. Under that convention, four of the seven instruction-
tuned models drop below 3.5σ on harmful content (their CI
lower bound is <3.5), reinforcing that the structural signal is
more uncertain than single-run reporting suggests.
